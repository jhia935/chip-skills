# Relaxor Ferroelectric

A class of ferroelectric materials characterized by:
- **A broadened, frequency-dependent dielectric peak** (T_m rather than a sharp Curie temperature)
- **No long-range ferroelectric order** at zero field — only short-range polar correlations ([[polar-nanoregions-pnr|PNRs]])
- **Field-induced ferroelectric behavior**: applying a strong DC bias can convert the relaxor into a long-range ferroelectric, then it reverts on field removal

Originally observed in lead-based compositions (PMN, PMN-PT), the field has shifted aggressively toward **lead-free relaxors** including Ba(Ti,Zr)O₃, (Ba,Ca)(Ti,Zr)O₃ ("BCTZ"), and the **B-site-Ca-doped BaTiO₃** that [[he-2025-bsite-ca-batio3-pnr|He et al. 2025]] characterize.

## Why relaxors are interesting for MLCCs

- **High ε_r over a broader temperature range** — flattened peak helps meet wide [[temperature-coefficient-of-capacitance|TCC envelopes]] like X8R / X8L / X9R.
- **Better DC-bias stability** — no large domains to align means [[dc-bias-derating|`f_V` is shallower]].
- **Reduced [[aging-class-2|aging]]** — no long-range order to age.

The trade-off is typically lower ε_r at the dielectric maximum compared to a sharp ferroelectric peak. Modern formulations bridge the gap.
