# Detection of Cracks in MLCCs by X-ray Imaging

**Authors:** C. Andersson, J. Ingman, et al.
**Venue:** *Microelectronics Reliability* (Elsevier), 2016.
**Type:** T3 peer-reviewed paper — quantitative X-ray FA study.

## Summary

A systematic test of **X-ray imaging as a non-destructive crack detector** for MLCCs. **300 MLCCs** were **bent to varying levels of strain**, imaged by **2D and 3D (CT) X-ray**, and then **cross-sectioned to verify** what the X-ray showed. Parts were mounted in **different orientations relative to the bending axis** to map detectability vs crack geometry.

## Key findings

- X-ray reveals many bend-induced (flex) cracks, but **tightly closed cracks are hard to see** in conventional 2D — detection depends on the crack opening enough to create **density/attenuation contrast**.
- **3D CT** improves localization and orientation-independence over single-projection 2D.
- **Cross-section DPA** remained the ground-truth method to confirm X-ray calls.

## Significance

Establishes the **inherent limit of X-ray** for MLCC crack FA: a tight, unopened crack (or tightly closed delamination) can be radiographically invisible. This is why acoustic methods — which respond to the *interface*, not the density — are preferred for closed defects (see [[acoustic-microscopy-tight-delaminations-mlcc]]).
