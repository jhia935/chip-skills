# Acoustic Microscopy of Tightly Closed Delaminations in MLCCs

**Type:** T3 peer-reviewed paper (authors/venue not fully resolved in capture — cite by title).

## Summary

Addresses the **hardest defect to find in an MLCC: a tightly closed delamination**. **Scanning acoustic microscopy (C-SAM)** is the established, preferred **non-destructive** method for delamination/crack/void detection, and **DPA cross-sectioning correlates well in most cases** — *but* when a delamination is **tightly closed**, both routes fail: X-ray sees no density contrast, and once the sample is sectioned the thin gap collapses, so **optical/SEM is inconclusive**.

The paper introduces **Rayleigh-wave acoustic imaging performed on the physically sectioned surface**. Surface (Rayleigh) waves are sensitive to **tightly closed delaminations and near-surface flaws**, recovering defects that disappear under conventional post-section microscopy. It is positioned as a **verification technique for conventional C-SAM data**. Acoustic methods of this family can resolve gaps **down to ~0.1 µm**.

## Significance

The methodological keystone for the **"detection limit vs defect openness"** theme: defect *openness*, not just size, governs which modality can see it. Tightly closed → acoustic (Rayleigh/C-SAM); open with density contrast → X-ray/CT; root cause → DPA. Cataloged on [[mlcc-failure-analysis-techniques]].
