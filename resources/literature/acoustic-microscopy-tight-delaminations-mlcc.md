# Acoustic Microscopy of Tightly Closed Delaminations in Multilayer Ceramic Chip Capacitors

- **URL:** https://www.academia.edu/23498873/Acoustic_Microscopy_of_Tightly_Closed_Delaminations_in_Multilayer_Ceramic_Chip_Capacitors
- **Type:** T3 peer-reviewed paper (authors/venue not fully resolved in capture). Captured 2026-06-24 from web research (abstract + secondary FA summaries; full text not held — do not over-attribute).

## Key claims (as captured)
- **Scanning acoustic microscopy (C-SAM)** is a well-established, **non-destructive** method to evaluate MLCCs for **delaminations, cracks, and voids**; it is the **preferred method for delamination detection**.
- Destructive physical analysis (**DPA, cross-sectioning**) correlates well with the non-destructive evaluation **in most cases**.
- **However, when delaminations are tightly closed**, optical and SEM examination of sectioned samples may be **inconclusive** (the gap is too thin to resolve once sectioned).
- The paper introduces an acoustic micro-imaging technique — **Rayleigh-wave imaging** — performed on the **surface of the physically sectioned sample**. It is sensitive to **tightly closed delaminations** and near-surface flaws, and is "**ideally suited for verification of conventional acoustic micro imaging data**."
- Ultrasonic/acoustic microscopy can reveal gaps **as small as ~0.1 µm** (from the broader FA literature captured alongside this paper).

## Notes
- The core methodological contribution: tight (closed) delaminations defeat both X-ray (no density contrast) and post-section optical/SEM (gap collapses) — Rayleigh-wave imaging on the cut face recovers them.
- Anchors the "detection-limit by defect openness" theme on the FA-techniques page.
